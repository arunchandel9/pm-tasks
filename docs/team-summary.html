MangoEyes Task Review Summary

Every client request, from any channel, becomes the right task on the right board with the sheet kept in sync. Nobody has to watch the channels to make that happen.

Today
  • Requests arrive on Slack, email, WhatsApp, ad platforms, and in meetings.
  • A PM has to notice each one, work out what is asked, and create the card by hand.
  • The same ask on two channels becomes two cards. Meeting actions get lost.
  • The PM sheet drifts from the board. Nobody can ask "what is open for this client" and trust the answer.
With the Task Hub
  • Everything lands in one place. Client and team are worked out automatically.
  • Clear requests become a Pulp card and a sheet row in under a minute, with an internal acknowledgement on the original message.
  • Unclear ones go to one Slack channel where a person clicks Approve or Edit.
  • When a card moves, the sheet follows. One end-of-day summary. Anyone can ask the hub a question from their own assistant.

What happens to a request

Read it left to right. Orange is automatic. Green, dashed, is the one place a person decides.

One message, start to finish

A client writes in their Slack channel: "The Book Now button on the contact page isn't working on mobile, and can we also get the new price list up this week?"

  1. It lands in the hubThe channel belongs to Clinic X, so the client is known without any guessing. The message is stored with a link back to it.
  2. It is checked against open workNothing similar for Clinic X in the last 14 days, so it continues. If the same ask had come by email an hour earlier, it would attach to that one and reply "already tracked".
  3. It is readTwo separate asks are found: a broken button on mobile, and a price list update. Each with the client's exact words.
  4. Team and type are worked outAsk 1: a dev issue for the Dev team. Ask 2: a content update for the Content team. Both clearly. Each gets a drafted title, description, and labels.
  5. The rules say where it goesDev issues go to the Dev board, content updates to the Content board. "This week" is noted as a priority hint.
  6. A person reviewsIn the first two weeks, both drafts appear in #pm-review. A PM clicks Approve on each. Later, clear cases like these skip this step.
  7. It is createdTwo Pulp cards with owner, priority, and due date, two sheet rows, and a ✅ reaction on the client's message. Nothing is sent to the client automatically; a PM replies when they choose.
  8. It stays in syncWhen the developer moves TASK-131 to Done in Pulp, the sheet row updates within seconds and it appears in that evening's summary.

After the card exists

The hub remembers everything: every message, every task, every stage change. Pulp and the sheet are kept up to date from it, never the other way round. That is what makes it trustworthy.

Meetings, client and internal

Everything that comes in is either client work or internal work. The hub decides which from where it came from: a client's channel, a client's email domain, a meeting with a client attendee. It never guesses. If it cannot tell, the item goes to review marked "unknown client" and a person picks.

Client meetings

  • Our tasks become cards on the client's boards.
  • Client to-dos ("they'll send the logo Monday") are tracked as "client owes us" and show in the daily summary until they arrive.
  • Decisions go to a decision log, linked to the meeting, so "what did we agree about X" has an answer.
  • Meetings are always reviewed, as one batch: "8 tasks from the call with Clinic X: approve all, or edit".

Internal meetings and channels

  • Tasks go to the internal board, with whoever was named as owner.
  • Ideas go to an ideas backlog, never onto a task board. They resurface in a weekly summary so they are not lost.
  • Decisions go to the same decision log.
  • The daily summary has a client section and an internal section. The sheet has an Internal tab.

Asking the hub

Connect your own assistant, such as Claude or Codex, to the hub once. Then ask in plain words: "What's open for Clinic X and what's blocked?" "Draft a status update for Clinic Y from this week's movement." "Chart tasks created per client this month." Your assistant does the thinking and the visuals. The hub only answers truthfully from the record. Nothing a client sees is changed without a person in the loop.

How much you review, and when it changes

  • Weeks one and two: every drafted task goes to #pm-review, even the obvious ones. You approve, edit, or dismiss. This is deliberate. Those clicks tell us exactly where the hub can be trusted.
  • After that: clear cases are created directly and you see them in the daily summary. Only unsure cases, possible duplicates, and anything with an unknown client still come to review.
  • Always reviewed: meetings. A transcript is less certain than a written request, and a batch of wrong cards on a client board is expensive.

What effort cannot compress

  • Google's side of meetings. Reading full transcripts through Google's event system needs a Cloud project, consent screens, and a Workspace admin, which can take an afternoon or a week. So day one uses the simpler path: the Gemini notes doc lands in a Drive folder after every call and the hub reads that folder every few minutes. Full transcripts are a later upgrade if notes are not enough.
  • The review gate. It opens on evidence, not a date: per request type, once 30 approvals have gone through with at least 95% unedited, and never sooner than 3 days. With real volume that is about a week. Meetings always stay reviewed.

Removed so they cannot block: Pulp's card-moved webhook (polled every minute until your developer adds it) and any embeddings service (dedupe is done in the database). Could still add hours: a client channel in the client's own Slack workspace, a Pulp corner that differs from Trello, or Google access held by someone not in the room.

What we need from the team

  • Access to Pulp and to the PM sheet.
  • The client list: name, Slack channel(s), email domain(s). Plus the list of internal channels.
  • Confirmation that client Slack channels live in the MangoEyes workspace.
  • The intake mailbox address, or a go-ahead to pick one.
  • New habits from day one: CC the mailbox on client emails, forward WhatsApp asks, share stray messages to #intake, turn on note-taking in client calls.
